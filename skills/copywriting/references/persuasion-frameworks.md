# Persuasion Frameworks

The five structures this skill and its siblings write with. Pick the structure from the reader's awareness first (`SKILL.md` §2); this file holds each structure's steps, what it fits and where it breaks. Framework and trigger names never appear in the deliverable.

## PAS: Problem, Agitate, Solve

**Problem**: the pain, in the reader's own words. **Agitate**: what it costs them (the hidden cost, the consequence, what it blocks). **Solve**: the product as the way out, with one proof point and one CTA.

Fits problem-aware readers: landing pages, cold email openers, feed posts.
**Fails when** it agitates a pain the reader doesn't already feel (melodrama), or presses on health, money or personal distress (exploitation). In B2B, agitate with the cost of inaction in the reader's own numbers.

## AIDA: Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

**Attention**: something true and specific to them. **Interest**: something they didn't know about their world. **Desire**: the outcome and the proof that it happens. **Action**: one CTA.

Fits broad audiences with mild interest: homepage heroes, sequence openers, feed posts.
**Fails when** "Attention" becomes an unsupported superlative or a shock claim the body can't pay off.

## BAB: Before, After, Bridge

**Before**: their reality, concrete. **After**: a believable outcome. **Bridge**: the mechanism that gets them there, in brief.

Fits transformation products, case studies, carousels, before-and-after creative.
**Fails when** the After is one typical customers don't reach: show the typical result, or a named customer's with their consent.

## 4Ps: Promise, Picture, Proof, Push

**Promise**: a measurable claim you can back. **Picture**: them, with the result. **Proof**: from the proof inventory. **Push**: a true reason to act now, or just the CTA.

Fits product-aware readers when skepticism is the bottleneck: retargeting, pricing and upgrade pages, late-funnel email.
**Fails when** Push turns into manufactured urgency. No real deadline means no deadline.

## SSS: Star, Story, Solution

**Star**: a real customer the reader identifies with, who agreed to be the story. **Story**: their struggle and turning point, with their own specifics. **Solution**: what changed, shown more than told.

Fits unaware readers, who must recognize themselves before they see a problem: testimonial sections, customer stories, creator and UGC scripts, case-study emails.
**Fails when** the Star is invented, composite, AI-generated or an undisclosed insider: that is a fake testimonial. No real Star yet: `[SOURCE NEEDED: customer story]`.

## Triggers

The ten triggers (loss aversion, social proof, scarcity, urgency, authority, reciprocity, curiosity gap, anchoring, novelty, identity), each with its phrasing, evidence grade and the condition under which it is true, live in `references/psychology/guide.md` § Lever cards. Use at most two per decision moment (a hero, a pricing block, a CTA), one per barrier the evidence shows.

Ads compress these structures into ad formats in the ad-creative capability. Email and social use them only when the message has to persuade (`references/email/copy-guidelines.md`, `references/social/post-templates.md`). Paywall structure and conversion diagnosis belong to the cro capability (each if available).
