# Social Metrics

Measure who engaged and what they did next, against the account's own history. Followers and engagement rate are context, never the goal.

## Goal → metric

| Goal | Primary metric | Supporting | Never the target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reach new ICP | Non-follower reach; new followers who fit the ICP | Profile visits | Impressions, likes |
| Convert | Profile and link clicks per post; signups or demos tagged to social | Replies asking how to try it | Engagement rate |
| Learn | Substantive replies and DMs from ICP | Questions you hadn't heard | Comment count |
| Hire | Applications that cite the post | Profile visits from candidates | Reach |
| Launch | Signups and activated accounts from each launch source | Questions in the thread | Upvotes, points, rank: outcomes, never asks |

## ICP share

For each post, read the first 20 engagers (replies, reposts, likes) and mark each ICP, peer or other from their title, company and bio. Track the share per pillar; the decision rule is in `references/social/guide.md` § Engage and measure.

## Tiers against the account's median

Take the median of the goal metric over the last 20-30 posts on the same account, platform and format:
- 5× or more: an outlier; name its asset and angle, and re-angle it once.
- 2-5×: strong; a repurposing candidate.
- 0.5-2×: normal; change one variable at a time.
- Below 0.5×: weak; check the topic and the ICP share before the hook.

Engagement rate falls as an account grows, so compare like with like and never across platforms.

## Attribution floor

Most social influence leaves no clickable trail: screenshots, DMs, "a colleague mentioned you". Count two things and treat both as a floor, not the total:
- UTM parameters on every link you control (`utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=<pillar or launch>`).
- A free-text "How did you hear about us?" field at signup or demo booking, coded monthly.

Funnels, cohorts and attribution models belong to the product-analytics capability, if available.

## External benchmarks

Published averages differ by denominator (followers, reach or impressions), account type (company or person) and year, so they can't set targets. If you quote one, give its source, date, denominator and account type, and compare it only with the same denominator on your own account.
