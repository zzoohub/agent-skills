# Generative Production

Scope: when AI belongs in ad production, how to choose a tool, what it costs, and the checks before export. Labelling rules: `references/ad-policy-compliance.md`.

## When it helps, when it hurts

- **Helps**: backgrounds and scenes around a real product shot; resizes and outpainting to new ratios; new openings re-cut onto a proven video; localization (dubbing, captions, on-screen text); animatics before a shoot; static variants of a proven concept.
- **Hurts**: synthetic "customers" giving testimonials (barred in the US by 16 CFR 465 and rejected by platforms when undisclosed); renders of the product that differ from what ships; a generic AI look in formats where authenticity is the proof, such as creator and UGC ads.

## Choosing a tool

Choose by capability, not brand: reliable in-image text; native audio and lip sync; clip length and shot-to-shot consistency of people and product; edit control (inpainting, reference images); commercial-use terms and indemnity. Models, their limits and their shutdown dates turn over within months: take the current model and its deprecation date from the vendor's own page at the time of use, never from memory. Storyboard video as short shots and stitch them, because maximum clip length keeps changing.

Budget by cost per usable asset: the vendor's unit price × attempts per keeper (measure it on the first batch), plus licence or seat minimums.

## Voice and likeness

- Written consent from anyone whose voice or likeness is cloned; check the platform's policy on cloned voices; keep a library of approved voice profiles.
- A real creator's face or voice never says what they did not say or approve.

## QC before export

The product looks exactly as it ships; no warped hands, faces or text; no characters, music or marks you hold no rights to; synthetic people, voices and realistic scenes labelled; legal lines and disclosures present; VO pronunciation checked by a native speaker for each language; captions on.
