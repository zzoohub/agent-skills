# Claims: Research, Hard Cases, Market Rules, Disputes

Depth for Stage 2; the rules themselves are in SKILL.md.

## Research order

Fetch in this order, logging each source as you go (URL, check date, and a capture for prices and plan gates): in the page's sources block, or as register rows in a program. [Your Product]'s facts come from its live pricing page and docs to the same standard (in a program, its own register file); the price book (`biz/marketing/pricing.md`) may hold unreleased prices.

| Source (what it yields) | Watch for |
|---|---|
| Pricing page and plan matrix (prices, seat minimums, billing periods, plan gates) | Annual prices shown per month; "from" prices that need add-ons for parity; regional prices |
| Help center and docs (limits, quotas, add-ons) | Limits that differ by plan; legacy plans still documented |
| Changelog and release notes | A ✗ on something that shipped after the reviews you read |
| Terms of service and trial terms (benchmark "DeWitt" and competitive-use clauses) | Read before any hands-on test; if testing or publishing results is barred, no performance claim |
| Reviews (G2, Capterra, forums), last 12–18 months: triggers (1–3★), where they win (4–5★) | Count, rating band and date window per theme; one loud thread is not a trend. Each site's vendor rules bind what you reuse: G2 bars republishing another vendor's reviews or star rating; Gartner Peer Insights bars 1:1 rating comparisons (checked 2026-10-08); check any other site's terms first |
| Their comparison pages about you (what their rep will repeat) | Errors about [Your Product] to correct in sourced sentences, not a rebuttal section |
| Export and API docs (what leaves their product, in what format) | Feeds the F5 What Transfers table |

## Hard cases

- **"From" prices.** Weak: "[Your Product] from $8/user vs [Competitor] from $10/user", when this buyer needs SSO, which costs $16/user on [Your Product] and $15/user on [Competitor]. Strong: "For 25 users who need SSO, billed annually, [Your Product]'s Business plan costs $400/mo and [Competitor]'s Pro plan $375/mo (list prices, checked 2026-10-08)." Publish the comparison even when you lose it, and say why this buyer should weigh something else.
- **Crossovers.** [Competitor] costs $15/user a month, or $12 billed annually; [Your Product] costs $99 a month for up to 10 users, then $10/user. [Competitor] is cheaper up to 6 users on monthly billing and up to 8 on annual ($96 vs $99 at 8); above that, [Your Product] is, at any size. A table at 10 and 25 seats alone misleads a 5-person team. Show 5, 8 and 25 seats, and state both crossovers.
- **Usage-based pricing.** Price the same 2–3 usage levels on both sides, assumptions stated; a per-unit rate alone hides minimums and overage.
- **"Contact sales" competitors.** Compare what is public: the pricing model (seat, platform fee, usage), minimums, contract term, onboarding fees and what each tier gates. A named third-party price dataset may be cited, attributed and dated, never shown as a list price, and goes to legal review.
- **A trigger with no count** is an opinion; label it as one.
- **Performance tests** publish their configuration so a third party can rerun them, and retire when either product ships a major release.
- **Market superlatives** ("most-used", "top-rated") show the independent source and its date on the page; a badge names its report period.
- **Testimonials:** endorsement and review law lives with the copywriting capability, if available.
- **Own-site roundups.** Presenting a site you control as independent reviews of a category that includes your product breaks 16 CFR 465.6, and Google asks for first-hand evidence behind any "best". Unaudited, correlational 2026 data: seven SaaS sites publishing self-ranked listicles lost 29–49% of Google visibility in late January, and AI Overviews citing such a listicle in September left its publisher out of the recommendation 83% of the time (Lily Ray, Feb and Oct 2026).

## Market rules

This flags issues for counsel; it is not legal advice.

| Market | Rules that bite | What they demand |
|---|---|---|
| US | Lanham Act §43(a)(1)(B) (competitor suits); FTC Policy Statement on Comparative Advertising (1979); 16 CFR 465.6; state false-advertising (UDAP) statutes; NAD challenges | Truthful, substantiated claims (truthful disparagement is lawful). Challenges usually arrive as a demand letter or an NAD case; the NAD refers non-compliers to the FTC |
| EU | Directive 2006/114/EC Art. 4, as transposed by each member state | Goods "meeting the same needs"; an objective comparison of "material, relevant, verifiable and representative features"; no denigration, confusion or unfair advantage from their mark |
| UK | Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008, reg. 4; CAP Code section 3 (ASA) | The EU directive's tests; the ASA rules on complaints about published pages |
| KR | 표시·광고의 공정화에 관한 법률 Art. 3(1); KFTC 비교표시·광고에 관한 심사지침 | A clear target and criteria under the same conditions; current figures (a competitor figure that changed before publishing is unfair even if you missed it); no overall superiority from selected wins; performance tests by an independent body; a true comparison is still disparagement when its overall effect makes them look markedly worse than they are |

Sources, checked 2026-10-08: law.cornell.edu (15 U.S.C. 1125; 16 CFR 465.6) · ftc.gov (comparative-advertising policy statement) · bbbprograms.org (NAD) · legislation.gov.uk (2006/114/EC Art. 4; SI 2008/1276 reg. 4) · asa.org.uk (CAP Code section 3) · law.go.kr (KFTC 예규 제269호, 비교표시·광고에 관한 심사지침).

## Disputes

When anyone says a claim is wrong:

1. Re-verify it against the current source within 2 business days.
2. If it is wrong or stale, correct the page with a dated note ("Corrected 2026-10-12: [Competitor] now includes SSO on Business"), update the sources line or register row, and reopen every page that uses it.
3. If it is right, reply privately with the source URL, the capture and the check date.
4. Never argue in public. Legal letters and NAD or ASA notices go to counsel.
