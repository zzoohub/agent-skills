# Product Brief Examples

Weak lines next to better ones, all from one idea: a booking tool for independent yoga and pilates studios, whose owners lose income when a booked client doesn't come.

## Problem: a solution in disguise

**Weak:** "Small studios need a modern, affordable booking app with waitlists and automatic reminders."

**Better:** "When a booked client doesn't come, the spot stays empty and earns nothing. Owners of one-room studios describe 2–4 booked-but-empty spots in each peak class [Said: 6 of 8 owners, about last week's classes]. Two keep a paper waitlist and text people by hand when someone cancels [Observed: seen at the front desk, 2 studios]."

The weak line names a product, and would read the same if nobody had the problem. The better one stays true whatever gets built, and a reader can check each claim.

## Evidence: enthusiasm graded as proof

**Weak:** "Validated: 9 of 10 owners said they would pay $50 a month [Observed]."

**Better:** "9 of 10 owners said they would pay $50 a month [Assumed: future intent, asked after the pitch]. One pays $139 a month for a suite she uses only for bookings [Observed: her invoice]. Nobody has committed money to this."

What people say they will do costs them nothing. What one owner already pays is the only price evidence here.

## Riskiest assumption: the comfortable one

**Weak:** "Risk: larger competitors may copy the waitlist. Next step: build an MVP and gather feedback."

**Better:**

| # | If false, the idea dies | Evidence now |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A spot freed by a cancellation gets taken before class, if the waitlist hears in time | [Said: 1 of the 2 owners who text by hand, "about half get taken"] |
| 2 | Owners will pay to have that done for them | [Assumed]; one pays $139 a month for a suite she uses only for bookings [Observed: her invoice] |
| 3 | Owners can be reached without paid ads | [Assumed] |

**Next test (row 1):** take over the texting by hand for 3 studios recruited from one instructors' forum, messaging the waitlist as each cancellation comes in · Kill: fewer than 1 in 4 freed spots taken · Pass: at least half taken, and 2 of the 3 owners pre-pay three months at $40 · 4 weeks, about 5 hours a week

"Competitors may copy it" is a worry about success, and "gather feedback" can't fail. Row 1 is the belief everything else rests on, held up by one owner's word. The test needs no software, recruits through the channel the brief names, and asks for money, so one run touches all three rows. Its numbers were set first.

## Success Signal: a KPI with a deadline

**Weak:** "Reduce no-shows by 30% within six months of launch."

**Better:** "Owners stop keeping a paper waitlist. A spot freed by a cancellation is taken by someone else before the class starts, without the owner sending a message."

The brief says what would be seen if it works. The target, the date and what must not get worse belong to the PRD, once there is a baseline to set them against.

## Decision: a verdict the grades don't support

**Weak:** "Decision: Pursue → PRD: strong interest across the market."

**Better:** "Decision: Test first: the problem is [Observed] in 2 studios and [Said] in 6, but that a freed spot gets refilled rests on one owner's word, and nobody has paid."

Four weeks later the same line is updated, not rewritten: "Decision: Pursue → PRD: 41 of 68 freed spots were taken [Observed: our message log, 3 studios] and 2 owners pre-paid three months [Committed]."
